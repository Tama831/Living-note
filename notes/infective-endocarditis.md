## 📌 Five-line summary
<!-- LN:SUMMARY:START -->
- The typical IE patient has shifted from "young, with structural heart disease" to "elderly, multimorbid, healthcare-associated", and *S. aureus* is now the leading pathogen. The disease remains heavy: in-hospital mortality 15–25%, 1-year mortality near 40%, surgery required in 40–50%.
- With the spread of TAVI, M-TEER and sutureless valves, prosthesis-related IE has become the new battleground. Incidence is low, but diagnosis is hard and the affected population overlaps with those who can least return to surgery — so outcomes are poor.
- On diagnosis, a meta-analysis reconfirmed the superiority of TEE, while metagenomic sequencing steps in for culture-negative cases and multimodality imaging for fungal and device-related disease.
- Right-sided IE linked to injection drug use is rising, and coexisting PFO raises the problem of paradoxical embolism. Percutaneous mechanical aspiration (PMA) entered an AHA advisory as a source-control option for high-surgical-risk patients.
- Therapeutic attention is widening to multidisciplinary endocarditis teams, long-acting dalbavancin, modifiable prognostic factors such as preoperative albumin, and the adjunctive effects of statins.

## 🧵 Cross-cutting synthesis
<!-- LN:SYNTHESIS:START -->

### A tectonic shift in epidemiology — who gets this disease has changed

Since its first description in the late 19th century, the epidemiology of IE has changed shape
dramatically. Once a disease of relatively young patients with structural heart disease, it now
centers on elderly patients with multiple comorbidities and on infections related to healthcare
itself ([Rapti 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42515024/)). The pathogen league table
shifted in step: *Staphylococcus aureus* has displaced viridans streptococci at the top. The two
drivers named again and again are rising injection drug use and healthcare-associated infection
([Bogni 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42405366/)). Global Burden of Disease data report
that absolute case numbers are trending upward — repositioning IE from "rare but severe" to
"increasingly common and severe" ([Di Vito 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42041981/)).

The severity, meanwhile, has not been updated downward. In-hospital mortality is 15–25%, 1-year
mortality approaches 40%, and 40–50% of cases need surgery
([Ishak 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42192704/) /
[Abd Rani 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42469863/)). The fact that mortality has not
fallen while the epidemiology transformed is the background pressure behind every theme below.

### Valves are not equal — site-specific structural vulnerability

IE does not distribute evenly across valves. Aortic 30–45%, mitral 25–30%, tricuspid 5–10%,
pulmonary <2%, prosthetic 10–30% — a skew that is no accident, reflecting each valve's structure,
hemodynamics and shear stress. A narrative review organizes the view that this distribution maps
directly onto surgical complexity (access to annular abscess and perivalvular destruction) and
onto postoperative outcomes ([Abd Rani 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42469863/)). It is a
framework that exposes the limits of quoting a single number for "the prognosis of IE", and it
connects to the handling of right-sided IE below.

### IE in the device era — TAVI, TEER, sutureless valves

The rapid expansion of transcatheter therapy has brought IE a new phenotype. TAVI-related IE,
though relatively infrequent overall, stands out as a clinical problem with high morbidity and
mortality once it occurs ([Dădârlat-Pop 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42499544/)). Mitral
transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (M-TEER) devices share the pattern, and for both TAVR and TEER
the recurring keynotes are "a multidisciplinary approach so diagnosis is not delayed" and
"surgical intervention in selected cases" ([Aoun 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42489383/)).
On the surgical side, the Perceval sutureless valve — popular for its hemodynamics and shorter
operative times — carries the same problem, with incidence, diagnostic difficulties and management
now summarized as an expert-opinion review ([Rello 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41897624/)).

The dilemma common to all three is structural. Prosthetic material degrades the echocardiographic
window and makes diagnosis harder, while the patients who received these devices are precisely the
"elderly, high-surgical-risk, hence minimally invasive" population. The group hardest to diagnose
is the group least able to return for surgical rescue — the crossroads where this theme meets
fungal IE and imaging.

### Diagnosis — the echo hierarchy, and from culture to metagenomics

For echocardiography, a meta-analysis directly comparing TEE and TTE re-quantified the superiority
of TEE for detecting IE ([Yang & Zhang 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42088705/)). TEE has
limits of its own in prosthetic-valve and device cases, and multimodality imaging fills that gap.
A review of fungal IE (FIE) — against the backdrop of aging, prosthetic valves, implanted cardiac
devices and immunosuppression — organizes the role of imaging for a phenotype that is rare but
inherently difficult on both the diagnostic and therapeutic fronts
([Kalinski & Xu 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42428660/)).

On the microbiological side a more fundamental transition is under way. The paradigm shift from
culture toward next-generation sequencing / metagenomics is closing the long-standing gap of
culture-negative IE; one review frames IE as "the world's third most common fatal infection" while
mapping its clinical burden ([ElHefnawi 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42085838/)). Further
down the same road sits a precision-medicine vision that runs from comprehensive risk profiling —
genetic predisposition included — to early detection and individualized management
([Casallas-Barrera 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42017938/)). For now the vision runs
ahead of the evidence: validation on clinical outcomes is still to come.

### Right-sided IE and injection drug use — PFO, percutaneous mechanical aspiration

Injection drug use (IVDU) is a major risk factor for IE, with right-sided IE (RSIE) most frequent.
A meta-analysis has now quantified the valve distribution and the relative risk of RSIE, giving
contour to previously vague numbers ([Le 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42121066/)).
Layered on top is the problem of patent foramen ovale (PFO): the combination of "injection drug
use + RSIE + PFO" has been systematically reviewed as a syndrome producing systemic embolic events
([Cuervo 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42169692/)). There is, in other words, a population
in which the intuition "right-sided, so no need to worry about cerebral embolism" fails — a finding
that pushes the threshold for PFO work-up in RSIE downward.

On the treatment side, percutaneous mechanical aspiration (PMA) has stepped forward: catheter
technology originally developed for thrombus extraction, repurposed for vegetation source control.
An AHA science advisory positions it for patients responding inadequately to antimicrobials or at
high surgical risk ([El Sabbagh 2026a](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42095279/)), with a focused
companion piece on tricuspid-valve IE (TVIE)
([El Sabbagh 2026b](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41988451/)). TVIE itself is increasing with
injection drug use and cardiac implantable electronic devices (CIEDs) — demand pulling in the
technology. Note that an advisory is an expert positioning document, not a comparative trial; the
evidence staircase is still on its lower steps.

### Treatment — new shapes of antimicrobial therapy, and modifiable prognostic factors

The liveliest antimicrobial debate concerns dalbavancin, the long-acting lipoglycopeptide. A
systematic review of its effectiveness, safety and dosing has drawn a comment-and-reply exchange
([Lohana 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42257986/) /
[Chaldoupis 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42429893/)) — the appeal of outpatient
parenteral therapy (OPAT) and shorter admissions on one side, unresolved optimal dosing and
evidence quality on the other. On the resistance front, a case series plus literature review of
vancomycin-resistant enterococcal (VRE) IE spanning 2000–2024 presents its epidemiology,
management and mortality predictors in consolidated form for the first time
([Cairns 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42235917/)).

In surgery and perioperative care, the focus is shifting from operative technique to **the state
in which the patient reaches the operating room**. A systematic review / meta-analysis linking
preoperative albumin to postoperative mortality points to preoperative optimization as an
actionable margin ([Thet 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42493841/)). Among adjuncts, the
pleiotropic effects of statins on embolic events and mortality have been meta-analyzed
([Hannachi 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41926297/)), including their possible effect on
IE incidence itself.

The operational answer that keeps recurring is the multidisciplinary endocarditis team — spanning
infectious diseases, cardiology, cardiac surgery, imaging and (in drug-use cases) addiction
medicine. A review mapping that structure and the unmet clinical needs
([Di Vito 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42041981/)) and a scoping review surveying the
path from diagnosis to treatment ([Bogni 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42405366/)) form
the core of this season's reviews.

### What the case reports show at the margins — rare pathogens, overlapping syndromes

Outside the reviews sit case reports that fit no textbook chapter. IE from *Streptococcus
sinensis* complicated by bacterial meningitis began with the atypical entrance of bilateral
lower-limb ischemia before echocardiography found mitral vegetations
([Yang L 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42136553/)). Pneumococcal IE in two fully
vaccinated Irish children confronts non-vaccine serotypes and host immunity — the first child
developed IE on the heels of multifocal osteomyelitis and septic arthritis
([Crowley 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41951923/)). In a dengue-endemic region, IE
following dengue infection produced multi-organ infarcts of brain, kidney, liver and spleen, a
brain abscess, and Horner syndrome ([Sulaiman 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41876992/)).

Of a different feather, the collection also includes avian IE in broiler chickens caused by
*Vagococcus fluvialis* ([Chacón 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42072033/)). Not direct
material for human practice, but *Vagococcus* is occasionally reported in human IE, making this a
One Health reference for detection and pathogenicity.

### Holes not yet filled

Stating this note's current biases explicitly:

- The 25 papers here are mostly reviews, meta-analyses and case reports — **no primary RCTs or
  large prospective cohorts**. Primary evidence on oral switch (the POET line) and surgical timing
  must be supplemented separately.
- **No comparison against Japanese epidemiology or the JCS guideline** yet. The weight of
  IVDU-related RSIE should differ substantially between Japan and the West; the picture cannot be
  imported as-is.
- PMA stands at the AHA-advisory stage, **without comparative trials against surgery or
  antimicrobials alone**.
- Not a single paper on antibiotic prophylaxis (e.g. before dental procedures) entered this
  collection round.
